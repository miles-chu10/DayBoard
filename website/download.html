Download

Get the DayBoard beta.

The latest beta is DayBoard 1.3.0 beta 19. DayBoard is free, and AI is free for 14 days. The app and its disk image are signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple, and the app updates itself.

What you need

  • A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) running macOS 14 Sonoma or later.
  • A Google account for Google Calendar, Google Tasks and Gmail, if you use them. Google sign-in may be limited to invited accounts.
  • For the optional AI features: Claude Code, the Codex app or CLI, or an API key from your AI provider.

Install

  1. Download the .dmg, open it and drag DayBoard into your Applications folder. If an older beta is there, replace it. Your DayBoard data stays in place.
  2. Open DayBoard from Applications. If macOS asks whether to open an app downloaded from the internet, click Open.
  3. Turn on your sources in Settings → Sources. The install guide covers Google sign-in.

Already on beta 12 or later? DayBoard installs new betas when you restart, so you don't need to download it again.

New in beta 19

DayBoard Pro turns on. Everything that isn't AI stays free. The Assistant, AI actions, dictation, AI parsing in Quick Capture and learned memories start with a 14-day trial on each Mac, counted from the first time you use one, and everyone who updates gets the full 14 days. After that, DayBoard Pro unlocks them for good.

To send a bug or an idea, choose Help → Send Feedback… in DayBoard. It opens an email to support with your DayBoard and macOS versions filled in.

Beta 18 started the MCP server as soon as its port is free and notarized the disk image. Beta 17 brought Home, sending and snoozing mail, the Assistant beside any view, and Notes. Read the guide for the controls and shortcuts.

Check the download

Only install DayBoard from this page or the official GitHub release. The SHA-256 checksums for the DMG, ZIP, blockmaps and update metadata are in SHA256SUMS.txt. If macOS ever says it can't check DayBoard for malicious software, don't open that copy.
